CHAPTER 24 - ASSEMBLING THE DETECTOR

The STEM Fair ended at 3 PM.

Our robot won second place, which meant we advanced to the regional competition at Marindale College. Mom was thrilled. Ms. Ehlo was thrilled.

I barely noticed.

All I could think about was the man in the dark jacket, and the fact that he knew we were looking for the same thing he was.

Marvin's warning echoed in my mind: *There are forces working against you.*

That night, the three of us met at Grandpa's workshop.

I'd told Grandpa that we were working on a "secret project" for school, and he'd agreed to let us use the workshop as long as we cleaned up afterward.

I spread the four device pieces out on the workbench.

Piece 1: The small black box with wires and circuits.
Piece 2: The larger device with more wires and a circuit board.
Piece 3: The complex device with a screen and buttons.
Piece 4: The core—the small, glowing sphere.

"Okay," Raven said. "How do we put these together?"

I pulled out Marvin's letters and read through them again, looking for clues.

One of the letters mentioned that the pieces would "fit together naturally" once we had all four.

I picked up Piece 1 and examined it.

There were small connectors on one side—metal pins that looked like they could slot into something.

Piece 2 had matching slots.

I connected them, and they clicked into place with a satisfying snap.

"It's like LEGO," Siggy said.

We connected Piece 3 to Piece 2, and then carefully inserted the glowing core into a small chamber at the center of Piece 3.

The moment the core was in place, the device hummed to life.

The screen on Piece 3 flickered, and text appeared:

**DETECTOR ONLINE.**
**CALIBRATION REQUIRED.**
**AWAITING INPUT.**

"Whoa," Siggy said.

Raven leaned in, examining the screen. "What does it mean, 'calibration required'?"

"I don't know," I said.

I pressed one of the buttons on the device.

The screen changed:

**CALIBRATE DETECTOR?**
**YES / NO**

I pressed YES.

The device beeped, and the screen filled with a loading bar.

**CALIBRATING... 10%... 20%... 30%...**

We waited, holding our breath.

**CALIBRATION COMPLETE.**

**DETECTOR READY.**

**POINT AT TARGET TO ANALYZE.**

"Point at target," I said. "What does that mean?"

"Try pointing it at something," Raven suggested.

I picked up the device and pointed it at the wall.

The screen flickered, and new text appeared:

**ANALYZING...**
**MATERIAL: WOOD AND DRYWALL.**
**DENSITY: NORMAL.**
**ANOMALY: NONE.**

"It's scanning things," Siggy said.

I pointed it at the workbench.

**MATERIAL: WOOD AND METAL.**
**DENSITY: NORMAL.**
**ANOMALY: NONE.**

"Okay," I said. "So it detects materials. But Marvin said it would 'reveal the truth that has been hidden from this world.' What truth?"

"Maybe it only works with the scepter," Raven said. "And the Orrery."

"Then we need to find the scepter," I said.

According to Marvin's note, the scepter was hidden "beneath the foundation of this estate, in the place where fire once forged steel."

Hammerstrike had been built on the site of Brimley's Forge, the old ironworks that used to supply tools and equipment to the silver mines.

If there was a foundry, it would be underground.

"We need to go back to Hammerstrike," I said.

"When?" Raven asked.

"Tomorrow. Before anyone notices."

"And how do we get in?" Siggy asked. "The gates are locked."

I thought about it. "There has to be another way in. A back entrance, or a gap in the fence."

"Or," Raven said, "we could ask someone who knows the estate."

"Like who?"

"Grandpa," she said, looking at me. "You said he knew Marvin. Maybe he knows about the foundry."

(scene break)

The next morning, I went to Grandpa's house.

He was in the kitchen, making pancakes.

"Morning," he said. "You hungry?"

"Starving."

He slid a plate across the table, and I dug in.

"Grandpa," I said between bites, "did Marvin Marindale ever talk to you about Hammerstrike? Like...the history of the place?"

Grandpa raised an eyebrow. "Why do you ask?"

"Just curious. I saw it yesterday at the STEM Fair, and it's such a cool building. I was wondering if there's, like, a foundry or something underneath it."

Grandpa sat down across from me, his expression thoughtful.

"There is, actually," he said. "Or there was. When the estate was built, they left part of the old forge intact. Marvin loved that—said it was important to preserve history. He even gave tours of the foundry once in a while, showed people the old furnaces and tools."

"Do you know how to get to it?"

"There's an entrance on the east side of the property, near the gardens. A set of stone steps leading down. But it's been locked up since Marvin died. The Marindale Foundation didn't want kids sneaking in and getting hurt."

"Do you think it's still there? The foundry?"

"I imagine so. Why?"

I hesitated, then said, "We're doing a project. For school. About local history. I thought it would be cool to see it."

Grandpa studied me for a long moment, and I had the feeling he knew I wasn't telling him the whole truth.

But he didn't push.

"Well," he said, "if you're going to sneak into a locked foundry, at least bring a flashlight. And don't do anything stupid."

I grinned. "I won't."

(scene break)

That afternoon, the three of us met at the east gate of Hammerstrike.

The estate was quiet. The STEM Fair was over, and the grounds were deserted.

We found the gap in the fence that Siggy had scouted earlier—a section where the iron bars had rusted through—and squeezed through.

The gardens were overgrown, filled with weeds and wildflowers. We followed a stone path toward the east side of the mansion, where Grandpa had said the entrance to the foundry would be.

And there it was.

A set of stone steps, half-hidden by vines, leading down into the ground.

At the bottom of the steps was a heavy wooden door with a rusty padlock.

"Great," Siggy said. "How do we get past that?"

Raven pulled a bobby pin out of her hair. "Let me try."

"You know how to pick locks?" I asked.

"YouTube," she said.

She knelt down and started working on the padlock, twisting the bobby pin in the keyhole.

After a minute, there was a click.

The padlock opened.

"Nice," Siggy said.

We pulled the door open and stepped inside.

The foundry was dark and cold, the air thick with the smell of rust and old smoke.

I turned on my flashlight, and the beam cut through the darkness.

The room was huge—a cavernous space filled with old equipment. There were massive furnaces, anvils, and rows of tools hanging on the walls. The floor was covered in soot and ash, and the ceiling was blackened from years of fires.

"This place is amazing," Siggy whispered.

"Where's the scepter?" Raven asked.

I pulled out my phone and checked Marvin's note again.

*The scepter is hidden beneath the foundation of this estate, in the place where fire once forged steel.*

"It's here somewhere," I said.

We spread out, searching.

I checked behind the furnaces, under piles of scrap metal, inside old crates.

Nothing.

Raven examined the walls, running her hands along the stone, looking for hidden compartments.

Siggy poked around near the anvils, lifting tools and peering into the shadows.

"Guys," he said suddenly. "Look at this."

We ran over.

Siggy was standing in front of a large stone slab set into the floor, near the center of the room.

Carved into the slab was a symbol: a spiral.

The same spiral that was in the floor of the Orrery Room.

"This is it," I said.

We knelt down and examined the slab.

There were small grooves along the edges, like it was designed to be lifted.

"Help me," I said.

The three of us wedged our fingers into the grooves and pulled.

The slab was heavy, but it shifted.

We pulled harder, and it slid to the side, revealing a small compartment beneath.

Inside was a long, metal rod—about three feet long, with intricate engravings along its length and a pointed tip at one end.

The scepter.

I reached down and picked it up.

It was cold and heavy, and it thrummed faintly in my hands, like it was humming with some kind of energy.

"We did it," Raven said quietly.

"Yeah," I said. "We did."

Now we just had to wait for the autumn equinox.

(scene break)

That night, I couldn't sleep.

The scepter was hidden under my bed, wrapped in a blanket. The detector sat on my desk, its screen dark.

Tomorrow was September 22nd.

The autumn equinox.

Midnight.

Everything Marvin had planned was leading to that moment.

But I still didn't know what we were going to find.

I thought about Marvin's letter: *The detector will reveal the truth that has been hidden from this world.*

What truth?

An alien invasion? A wormhole? Something else entirely?

I didn't know.

But tomorrow night, I would find out.
