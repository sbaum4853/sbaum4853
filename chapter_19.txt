CHAPTER 19 - THE CHILDREN'S MUSEUM

The Marindale Children's Museum sat on the edge of downtown, housed in what used to be part of the old Brimley's Forge complex. The building was brick, three stories tall, with big windows and a bright blue sign out front that read: *DISCOVER. CREATE. EXPLORE.*

Mom had taken Lila and me there a few times when we first moved to town. It was the kind of place where little kids could build with blocks, play with water tables, and pretend to run a grocery store. But there was also a maker space on the second floor with tools, 3D printers, and electronics kits.

That's where I figured we'd find the second piece.

The museum opened at 10 AM on Saturdays, and the three of us were waiting outside when the doors unlocked.

"Act natural," I said as we walked in.

"I am acting natural," Siggy said.

"You're walking like a cartoon spy."

"No, I'm not."

"You kind of are," Raven said.

Siggy straightened up and tried to walk normally, which somehow made it worse.

The front desk was staffed by a college-aged girl with bright pink hair and a name tag that said JADE. She smiled at us as we approached.

"Hey! Here for the open workshop?"

"Uh, yeah," I said. "Is that okay?"

"Totally. Just sign in here." She slid a clipboard across the desk. "The maker space is on the second floor. Have fun!"

We signed in and headed for the stairs.

The first floor was full of younger kids running around, their parents trailing behind them. We passed a giant foam block area, a room with a pretend kitchen, and a section where kids were building marble runs.

The second floor was quieter.

The maker space was a large, open room filled with workbenches, shelves of supplies, and stations for different activities. One corner had a 3D printer humming away. Another had a soldering station with a sign that said AGES 12+ ONLY.

A few other kids were scattered around the room, working on projects.

"Okay," Raven said quietly. "So where do we start?"

"Marvin's note said the piece is here," I said. "And we need a four-digit code to unlock it. 7-4-9-3."

"But unlock what?" Siggy asked, looking around. "A box? A locker?"

I scanned the room.

There were cabinets along the walls, labeled with things like WOOD, FABRIC, ELECTRONICS. There was a pegboard with tools hanging on it. And in the back corner, near the soldering station, there was a tall metal cabinet with a combination lock on it.

"There," I said, pointing.

We walked over.

The cabinet was old, painted gray, with scratches and dents from years of use. The combination lock had four dials, each one numbered 0 through 9.

"This has to be it," Raven said.

I glanced around to make sure no one was watching, then reached for the lock.

I set the dials to 7-4-9-3 and pulled.

The lock clicked open.

My heart skipped a beat.

I removed the lock and opened the cabinet door.

Inside, on the top shelf, was another small metal box, just like the one we'd found at Fort Juniper.

Siggy let out a low whistle. "No way."

I pulled the box out carefully and set it on a nearby workbench. Raven closed the cabinet door behind us.

The box had the same rusted metal exterior, the same latch on the front.

I opened it.

Inside was another device—this one slightly larger than the first, with more wires and a small circuit board attached. And beneath it was another folded letter.

I unfolded the letter and read it aloud:

---

*Congratulations.*

*You have found the second piece.*

*By now, you may be wondering what you are building. I cannot tell you everything—not yet. But I can tell you this:*

*The machine you are assembling is a detector. When complete, it will allow you to see what others cannot. It will reveal the truth that has been hidden from this world.*

*But the machine is useless without the final piece—the core. The core is hidden in the place where I spent my final days, in the room where the stars align.*

*To find it, you must first prove yourselves worthy. Complete all the challenges. Unlock all the clues. And when the time comes, you will know where to go.*

*But beware. There are forces working against you. They do not want you to succeed. They will do everything in their power to stop you.*

*Trust each other. Trust the knowledge you have gained. And above all, do not give up.*

*The fate of more than you know depends on it.*

*—M.M.*

---

I finished reading and looked up at Siggy and Raven.

No one said anything for a long moment.

Finally, Siggy said, "This is insane."

"A detector," Raven said quietly. "To see what others cannot."

"What does that even mean?" I asked.

"I don't know. But he said we have to complete all the challenges." She looked at me. "How many challenges are there in the game?"

"I don't know. We've only done six."

"Then we need to keep going," Raven said.

I carefully packed the second device into my backpack, along with the letter.

"We should get out of here," I said. "Before anyone notices."

We walked back toward the stairs, trying to act casual.

But as we reached the top of the staircase, I saw him.

The same man from the library.

Dark jacket. Sharp features. Cold eyes.

He was standing near the entrance on the first floor, talking to Jade at the front desk. He gestured toward the stairs, and Jade pointed up—right at us.

"Guys," I said, my voice tight. "We need to go. Now."

"What?" Siggy said.

"That guy from the library. He's here."

Raven looked down and saw him. Her face went pale.

"Back door," she said. "There has to be a back door."

We turned and hurried back into the maker space.

There was a door at the far end of the room, marked EXIT. We ran for it, weaving between workbenches and startled kids.

I shoved the door open, and we burst into a narrow stairwell. The stairs led down to a ground-level exit that opened into an alley behind the building.

We ran.

My backpack bounced against my shoulders, the weight of the two devices heavy and awkward. Behind us, I heard the exit door bang open.

He was following us.

"This way!" Raven shouted.

We turned a corner and sprinted down the alley, emerging onto a side street. Siggy's bike was locked to a rack near the front of the museum, but mine and Raven's were around the corner.

"Leave the bikes!" I shouted. "Just run!"

We ran three more blocks before ducking into a park and collapsing behind a cluster of trees.

I was gasping for breath, my heart hammering in my chest.

"Did we lose him?" Siggy panted.

I peered around the tree, scanning the street.

No sign of the man.

"I think so," I said.

"Who is he?" Raven asked, her voice shaking. "Why is he following us?"

"I don't know," I said. "But Marvin warned us. He said there are forces working against us."

"Tanthrop Hammond," Siggy said. "It has to be."

"But how did he know we'd be at the museum?" Raven asked.

I thought about it. "Maybe he's been watching us. Maybe he saw us at the library yesterday and followed us."

"Or maybe he knows about the clues," Siggy said. "Maybe he's trying to find the same things we are."

"Either way," I said, "we need to be more careful."

We sat there for a few more minutes, catching our breath and making sure we weren't being followed.

Finally, Raven said, "So what do we do now?"

I pulled out my phone and opened the group chat I'd started with her and Siggy.

"We text before we meet up," I said. "We make sure no one's following us. And we don't go anywhere alone."

"And we keep solving the challenges," Raven said.

"Right," I said. "Whatever Marvin was trying to warn us about, we need to figure it out. Before they do."

Siggy nodded. "So what's the next challenge?"

I thought about the game. We'd solved Challenge 6 with the Pythagorean Theorem. That meant Challenge 7 was next.

"I don't know," I said. "But we need to find out."
