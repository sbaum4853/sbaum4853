I still didn’t know what to do with Challenge Problem 6, but I decided it was late enough in the morning that I could text Raven. I pulled out my phone and started typing.  <text> Hi. It’s Gavin. Sorry I didn’t text last night. Got busy. </text> Weirdly, that text reminded me of when my mom called the dentist office after she forgot her appointment. I deleted it and tried again <text>  Hello. Gavin here. </text> Gavin here?  Was I a radio host?  I deleted and tried a third time. <text> Hi. It’s Gavin. Sorry I didn’t text last night. Siggy came over and brought this weird box of movies, or what we thought were movies </text> Nope.  That was already turning into the  longest text ever. Delete. Try again. <text> Hi. It’s Gavin. Sorry I didn’t text last night. Can I still come over today? </text> I clicked Send before I had a chance to chicken out. And then I waited.  I was about to set my phone down  and pretend I didn’t care  when the bubble appeared. Then a  reply . <text>  Of course you can! I’ve already told my mom you were coming for lunch and staying so we could read our books   </text> I reread it twice . Then I tapped  Like . Another text followed with her address and directions to her house.  She lived on Festival Lane. “Festival Lane,” I whispered to myself, an old memory from an adventure I once had on that street creeping up from the depths.  Festival Lane ran right alongside Rainier Park. It was just three blocks away I could walk if I wanted.  But I already had another idea. Festival Lane was in the same direction as Grandpa’s house.  I  headed to my room to load my backpack with the library books.  As I grabbed the stack off the desk, I noticed a little slip of paper on the floor in the corner, right by my bookcase. (show paper – close-up of an illustration from a few chapters back).  I  bent down and  picked it up.  ( show  paper – “A message is coming for you tonight. Take it seriously. This is all for real and I need your help.”) “What the heck?” I  said . I turned the paper over. Nothing on the back. I rotated it in my hands, studying the handwriting. I didn’t recognize  it. Now I kind of half-remembered when, last night, I dropped  Simple Robots for Clever Humans  on the floor. Had this paper fallen out from the book?  But that didn’t make any sense. If this paper  had been inside  that book… I stopped myself. Okay. No. I wasn’t doing this again.  I’d spent half of last night in a pseudo-freakout about Siggy’s box full of movie cases and I was glad to be done with all that. And yet—my thoughts kept going, because once they start, they don’t really ask permission. What if the “message”  was  Siggy’s box, and the Raspberry Pi inside?  The one that called us  Person  From  the Future .  The one that said aliens were trying to build a wormhole. And— I didn’t want to think the next thought. But I did anyway. The message on the Raspberry Pi said  I need your help. Didn’t it? I grabbed the Dragon Notebook and flipped pages until I found the sketch  I’d made while Siggy struggled with Challenge 4.  ( illustration : “Hello Person  From  the Future… I need your help.”) “No way,” I said aloud.  The idea that this slip of paper, which might have fallen out of a library book, was  connected in  any  way  to Siggy’s Raspberry Pi  made absolutely no sense. And yet my brain was right back where it had been the night before, stuck on the same impossible questions.  How did someone know  all of  Siggy’s favorite movies? How did they know he was the one kid in town who collected weird old movies on VHS? Who sent the box?  Why did it feel like things were lining up?  Person from the future . Wormholes. Once you let time travel into the mix, weird coincides stopped being impossible.  I shook my head, annoyed with myself. This was nonsense. The paper was vague. A message is coming for you tonight. That could mean anything, or nothing.  Library books were full of random old stuff.  Boomarks . Notes, Pressed flowers. This little note didn’t have to mean a thing.  And even if it had come from one of my books—which I didn’t know for sure that it did—it  definitely didn’t  mean aliens were real, or that someone was talking to us from the past. That was ridiculous.  These books had been sitting on a shelf in the Atlas Library for who-knows-how-long, and I’d just happened to check them out yesterday. There was no way someone could’ve planted a message for me inside one of them, timed perfectly to the night Siggy showed up with… …a weird a box of utter weirdness that we still didn’t understand.  As I zipped up my backpack, I  returned to the idea  that maybe Siggy was playing a prank on me. It didn’t feel like him. But it felt more likely than time travel. Whatever.  I’d figure it out.  I slung the backpack over my shoulder. “Mom!” I called. “I’m going to Grandpa’s, then to a friend’s house!”
