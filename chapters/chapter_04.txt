The front doors of the Atlas were enormous. Dark wood, polished smooth, they made me think of a giant’s castle, like I had just climbed to the top of the beanstalk . Grandpa pulled  on  one of the doors . It groaned as it  opened . We stepped inside.  The lobby was  vast.  And beautiful. And quiet.  An  open atrium with five  stories of iron catwalks circling upward, every floor lined with bookshelves that disappeared into the distance. Sunlight poured down from iron-framed windows near the ceiling. Thick iron beams formed a skeleton inside the brick walls, a reminder that this building, which was now quiet and calm, was once a booming factory of fire, hammers, and steel.  “Whoa,” I said.   “How come I never knew about this  place ?” “ The best-kept secret in every town is an old library in an old neighborhood,” Grandpa said. He pointed to something on the far wall. “Come here, look at this.”  Grandpa led me to a bronze plaque  mounted onto the brick wall . (sketch – in honor of Marvin Marindale…) “Marvin Marindale,” I  read . “That’s the guy you were talking about.” “Indeed,” said Grandpa.  “As you get older,  you’ll hear   stories about  Marvin, and I want you to know, whatever you hear about him, however crazy or eccentric  people make him out to be , he was a good man.  “ You knew  him?”  “ Oh yeah ,” Grandpa said, nodding. “Your dad knew him too.  Anyone  in town who  cared about science or engineering crossed paths with him eventually .” “Was he a scientist ,  or  was he an  engineer?”  I asked. Grandpa  chuckled . “Both. And neither.  Marvin  was a billionaire, and they  don’t have to pick one lane .” “ So why   would people say he was crazy ?” I asked. Grandpa waved the question away. “Don’t worry about that now. That story’s a couple hours long, at least, and the library closes in fifty-three minutes.” He patted my shoulder. “Go find a book.” “Wait, aren’t you  gonna  help me?” “ No sir,”  he  said , already turning toward a staircase . “ I’ll be in the basement. Best map collection in B rimley Forge  is down there. ” “But   I don’t know  my way around  this place.”    “You know how to use a library, don’t you?”  Grandpa called back as he disappeared down the stairs.  Classic Grandpa. Resigned that I had to talk to the librarian, I started toward the front desk, but then I heard a familiar voice from behind me.  “Gavin?” I turned. It was Raven  Schimmelfinney . “Oh, hey,” I said.  She tucked a lock of blue-streaked hair behind her ear.  “I thought that was you,” she said.  I didn’t even try to hide my relief at seeing her . At my new middle  school  I only  had  maybe three real friends total, and  Raven was one of them .  She sat next to me in math class.  I was glad to see a familiar face. “What are you doing here?” she asked. “I’m just… looking for a book.” She  grinned . “Aren’t we all?”  She  h ad a stack of hardback books under her left arm.  She pulled the top book from the stack and held it up to  show me. It had a shiny silver and red cover.  The title was  Daughter of the Moon-Crowned  Bloodstorm . “Weren’t you just reading that in class yesterday?” I  said . “Yesterday I was reading  Bride of the Shadow-Forged Tempest . That’s book eleven in the series. This is book twelve.” “Oh,” I said.  “What about you?” she said. “Reading anything  interesting ? ”   “I’m…trying to learn more about…electricity and…robotics.”      Strange  how hard it was to  say the words .  It was like they came out in slow motion.  I felt a little embarrassed.  It’s weird. S ometimes I feel like if I let other people know anything about me, like what I’m interested in, they might think I’m a total loser.  Not that Raven would ever be mean—she wasn’t like that at all.   She wouldn’t think  I was a weirdo  because I was  looking  for a robot ics  book at an old library on a Saturday afternoon.  She was a weirdo too, with a giant stack of books under her arm.  Still,  even knowing I was with a friend,  sometimes  it was hard for me to just be myself.  Sometimes, out of  nowhere , this  wave of  nervousness would  hit  me , like if  people knew who I really was they would think I was the dumbest dweeb  alive . “ Robotics?  That’s cool,”  Raven  said.  “Do you know where  to look?”   I shook my head. “I was about to ask the librarian.” “I bet we don’t even need to,”  said Raven . “Come here.” She led me across the lobby to a huge poster mounted on the stone wall beside a glass display case.  (show poster…shows Dewey Decimal classifications and what floor they’re on). I stared at the poster for maybe three seconds—just long enough to notice that the technology books had address number 600.  But then my eyes drifted  away from  the poster and  towards  the much more interesting display case right next to it.  It was a  short glass cabinet. Inside was a plastic model of a UFO, one that looked like it came straight from a 1950s sci-fi movie. A shiny silver disk with three spindly landing legs and a little bubble dome on top. Red and blue LEDs  lined the  edges, which I loved, because they were the same kind of LED’s Grandpa found in my robot kit.  A small placard inside  the case  read: NEW MEXICO: A HUB OF OUTER-SPACE IMAGINATION For generations, our state has fascinated scientists, storytellers, and sky-watchers hoping to learn whether we are alone in the universe. “They don’t have a  robots  category,” Raven  said. “I think it will be in technology,” I said, still looking at the case.  The bottom of the case was lined with black and white photographs, each one taped onto construction paper, like a science fair board at school. The photos showed glowing streaks in the night sky, newspaper clippings about “UNEXPLAINED SIGHTINGS,” and even a group of men gathered round a crater in the dirt.  “Technology, you’re right,” said Raven. “Six-hundreds. That will be on the fifth floor.”  “I just….one sec,” I said, and I  leaned  down to  get a closer  look at a placard in the corner.  THE “GREEN FIREBALL” SIGHTINGS NEAR NORTHERN NEW MEXICO LOCAL FOLKLORE: POSSIBLE CONTACT EVENTS This section of the display  had no photos;  instead  it had artwork that someone had  hand-drawn . The art showed green streaks of light in the night sky.  “Have you ever heard of this?” I  said . “Green lights in the sky  above Brimley Forge?”   “Really? When was this?”  Raven said. I looked closer at the newspaper article, scanning for a date.  “1948,” I said.  I felt disappointed as  soon  I said it. “That was a long time ago,” Raven said, echoing my thoughts.  UFO’s  were  cool  and all, but things that happened in 1948 might as well have happened when the dinosaurs were here.  “You said my book will be on the fifth floor?” I  said . Raven nodded. “Can you show me how to get there?  I’ve never been here before.” “Oh my gosh, you’re  gonna  love it here so much,” Raven said. “As far as I’m concerned, this is the most magical place on Earth.” And then she led me across the lobby to a winding staircase in the back.
