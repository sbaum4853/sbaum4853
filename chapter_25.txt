CHAPTER 25 - THE AUTUMN EQUINOX

Sneaking out of the house at 11:30 PM was easier than I expected.

Mom and Dad were asleep. Lila was asleep. Even Paige, who usually stayed up late practicing piano, had gone to bed early.

I grabbed my backpack—packed with the detector, the scepter, and a flashlight—and climbed out my bedroom window.

The night was cold and clear, the sky filled with stars.

I biked to the meeting spot: the park three blocks from Hammerstrike.

Siggy and Raven were already there, sitting on a bench under a streetlight.

"You made it," Raven said.

"Yeah. You?"

"My parents think I'm at a sleepover," she said.

"Same," Siggy said. "Sort of. I told them I was camping in the backyard. Then I just left."

We biked the rest of the way in silence, the town quiet and dark around us.

Hammerstrike loomed ahead, its windows black, the iron gates locked.

We slipped through the gap in the fence and made our way across the grounds, moving carefully, staying in the shadows.

The mansion was quiet. Empty.

We reached the back entrance—a service door that Raven had found unlocked during the STEM Fair—and slipped inside.

The hallway was dark, lit only by faint moonlight streaming through the windows.

We climbed the stairs to the second floor, then down the narrow hallway to the turret.

The door to the Orrery Room was still unlocked.

I pushed it open, and we stepped inside.

(scene break)

The Orrery Room was bathed in moonlight.

The brass planets of the orrery glinted as they rotated slowly, casting faint shadows on the walls.

In the center of the floor, the spiral indentation waited.

I checked my phone. 11:52 PM.

Eight minutes until midnight.

"Okay," I said. "Let's do this."

I pulled the scepter out of my backpack and walked over to the spiral.

The scepter fit perfectly into the indentation, sliding down until the pointed tip reached the bottom.

Nothing happened.

"Now what?" Siggy whispered.

"We wait," I said.

The minutes ticked by.

11:53.

11:54.

11:55.

At 11:58, the orrery began to move faster.

The planets spun, their brass arms clicking and whirring.

And then, at exactly midnight, the room filled with light.

It wasn't moonlight.

It was coming from the orrery itself—a brilliant, golden glow that radiated from the central sun.

The light traveled down the brass arms, illuminating each planet in turn, and then shot down through the spiral in the floor, following the path of the scepter.

The entire room was filled with shifting patterns of light and shadow, like a planetarium.

"Whoa," Siggy breathed.

The light converged on a single point on the far wall, near the bookshelf.

I ran over.

There, highlighted by the light, was a small engraving in the wood paneling.

Another spiral.

I pressed on it, and a hidden panel slid open, revealing a small compartment.

Inside was a piece of paper.

I grabbed it and unfolded it.

It was a map.

A map of the town, with a red X marked on a location just outside the downtown area.

Below the map, Marvin had written:

---

*The detector will show you what the map cannot.*

*Go to the marked location. Use the detector. And you will see the truth.*

*—M.M.*

---

I stared at the map, my heart pounding.

"Where is it?" Raven asked, looking over my shoulder.

"I don't know," I said. "It looks like...the old rail yard? The one by the river?"

"Why would Marvin send us there?" Siggy asked.

"I don't know. But he said the detector will show us the truth."

I pulled the detector out of my backpack and turned it on.

The screen flickered to life.

**DETECTOR READY.**
**POINT AT TARGET TO ANALYZE.**

I pointed it at the map.

**ANALYZING...**
**MATERIAL: PAPER AND INK.**
**ANOMALY: NONE.**

"Try pointing it at the wall," Raven suggested. "Where the light was shining."

I pointed the detector at the engraved spiral on the wall.

The screen flickered.

**ANALYZING...**
**MATERIAL: WOOD.**
**ANOMALY DETECTED.**
**RESIDUAL ENERGY SIGNATURE: EXOTIC MATTER.**
**SOURCE: NON-TERRESTRIAL.**

I froze.

"Non-terrestrial," Raven whispered. "That means—"

"Alien," Siggy said.

"Or," I said slowly, "from another dimension. Marvin was researching wormholes. What if the exotic matter he was talking about...what if it's real?"

"Then we need to go to the rail yard," Raven said. "Now."

(scene break)

The old rail yard was on the edge of town, a place where freight trains used to stop to unload supplies for the silver mines.

Now it was abandoned—a tangle of rusted tracks, crumbling platforms, and empty warehouses.

We biked there as fast as we could, the detector and the map tucked safely in my backpack.

When we arrived, the place was eerily quiet.

The moon hung low in the sky, casting long shadows across the tracks.

I pulled out the map and compared it to our surroundings.

"There," I said, pointing to an old warehouse near the far end of the yard.

We walked toward it, our footsteps crunching on the gravel.

The warehouse was dark, its windows broken, its doors hanging off their hinges.

Inside, the air was thick with dust and the smell of old wood.

I pulled out the detector and turned it on.

**DETECTOR READY.**
**POINT AT TARGET TO ANALYZE.**

I swept the detector across the room, watching the screen.

**ANALYZING...**
**MATERIAL: WOOD AND METAL.**
**ANOMALY: NONE.**

I kept moving, scanning the walls, the floor, the ceiling.

And then, in the far corner of the warehouse, the screen flashed.

**ANALYZING...**
**ANOMALY DETECTED.**
**RESIDUAL ENERGY SIGNATURE: EXOTIC MATTER.**
**SOURCE: NON-TERRESTRIAL.**
**WARNING: WORMHOLE APERTURE DETECTED.**

I stopped.

"Wormhole aperture," I said. "There's a wormhole here."

"Where?" Raven asked.

I pointed the detector at the corner of the room.

The screen zoomed in, showing a faint shimmer in the air—like heat waves rising off asphalt, but colder.

"There," I said. "Do you see it?"

Siggy squinted. "I don't see anything."

"Neither do I," Raven said.

But I could see it—just barely. A faint ripple in space, hovering a few feet off the ground.

"The detector is showing me something you can't see," I said.

I walked closer, holding the detector up.

The shimmer grew stronger, more defined.

And then I saw it.

A shape.

A ship.

(illustration: faint outline of an alien ship)

It was small—about the size of a car—and it was hovering in the corner of the warehouse, half-visible, like it was caught between two worlds.

"There's a ship," I said, my voice shaking. "An alien ship."

"What?!" Siggy said.

"I can see it with the detector. It's...it's not fully in our world. It's stuck in the wormhole."

Raven stared at the corner, her face pale. "Is it...dangerous?"

"I don't know."

I pointed the detector at the ship and pressed the button to analyze it.

The screen filled with data:

**ANALYZING...**
**MATERIAL: UNKNOWN ALLOY.**
**ENERGY SIGNATURE: EXOTIC MATTER.**
**STATUS: DORMANT.**
**THREAT LEVEL: LOW.**

"It's dormant," I said. "Whatever it is, it's not active."

"Why is it here?" Raven asked.

"I don't know. But Marvin knew about it. He built the detector to find it."

"And the invasion warning?" Siggy asked. "Was that real?"

I thought about it.

The video game. The clues. The letters.

Marvin had said there was a threat. But maybe the threat wasn't an invasion.

Maybe it was this.

A wormhole. A ship. A doorway to another world.

And maybe Marvin had been trying to protect it—or protect us from it.

"I think," I said slowly, "Marvin discovered this. And he didn't want Tanthrop Hammond to find it. So he hid it. And he left us clues so that if something happened to him, someone would know the truth."

"But why us?" Raven asked.

"Because we're kids," I said. "Because no one would take us seriously. We could find it and keep it secret."

"And now what?" Siggy asked. "Do we tell someone?"

"No," a voice said from behind us.

We spun around.

The man in the dark jacket was standing in the doorway of the warehouse.

(scene break)

He stepped into the light, and I got a good look at him for the first time.

He was tall, with sharp features and cold eyes. He wore a dark jacket and a badge clipped to his belt.

Tanthrop Hammond.

"You kids have done well," he said, his voice calm. "Better than I expected. But this is where it ends."

"Who are you?" I asked, trying to keep my voice steady.

"My name is Dr. Alan Finch. I worked with Marvin Marindale at Tanthrop Hammond. We were researching wormholes—stable apertures that could allow travel between dimensions. Marvin found this one. And then he hid it from us."

"Because you wanted to weaponize it," Raven said.

Finch smiled. "We wanted to study it. To understand it. But Marvin was paranoid. He thought we'd use it for the wrong reasons. So he sabotaged the project and disappeared. And now, thanks to you, I've found it."

He stepped closer.

"Hand over the detector," he said.

"No," I said.

Finch's expression hardened. "You don't understand what you're dealing with. That wormhole is dangerous. If it destabilizes, it could tear a hole in spacetime. The entire town could be destroyed."

"Then why do you want it?" Siggy asked.

"Because I can stabilize it. I can control it. But only if I have the detector."

I looked at Raven and Siggy.

We'd come too far to give up now.

"Run," I said.

And we ran.

(end of chapter 25)
