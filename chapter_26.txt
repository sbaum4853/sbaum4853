CHAPTER 26 - THE WORMHOLE CLOSES

We ran out of the warehouse and into the rail yard, our footsteps echoing on the gravel.

Behind us, I heard Dr. Finch shouting, "Stop! You don't know what you're doing!"

But we didn't stop.

We ran between rusted train cars, over cracked platforms, through tangles of weeds.

"Where are we going?!" Siggy shouted.

"I don't know!" I shouted back.

We reached the far end of the rail yard, where the tracks disappeared into a tunnel under the old highway overpass.

"In here!" Raven said.

We ducked into the tunnel, pressing ourselves against the wall.

It was dark and cold, the air thick with the smell of rust and mildew.

I pulled out my phone and turned off the screen, plunging us into darkness.

We waited.

Footsteps echoed through the rail yard.

Dr. Finch was searching for us.

"Come out," he called. "I'm not going to hurt you. I just need the detector."

I held my breath.

The footsteps grew closer.

And then they stopped.

"I know you're scared," Finch said, his voice calm. "But you need to trust me. Marvin was wrong. The wormhole isn't dangerous—it's a gift. A doorway to another world. And with the detector, we can finally unlock it."

I looked at Siggy and Raven in the darkness.

"He's lying," I whispered. "Marvin wouldn't have hidden this if it was safe."

"Then what do we do?" Raven whispered back.

I thought about it.

The wormhole was unstable. Finch had said so himself. And the detector could see it—could measure it.

What if we could close it?

What if that's what Marvin had wanted us to do all along?

I pulled out the detector and turned it on, keeping the screen dim.

**DETECTOR READY.**
**POINT AT TARGET TO ANALYZE.**

I opened the menu and scrolled through the options.

There were settings I hadn't noticed before: SCAN, ANALYZE, CALIBRATE...

And one that said: STABILIZE.

I clicked on it.

The screen changed:

**STABILIZATION MODE ACTIVATED.**
**WARNING: THIS WILL CLOSE THE WORMHOLE APERTURE PERMANENTLY.**
**CONFIRM? YES / NO**

I stared at the screen.

Close the wormhole.

That was it. That was what Marvin had wanted us to do.

Not to study it. Not to use it.

To close it. To protect the world from something we didn't understand.

I pressed YES.

The detector hummed, louder than before, vibrating in my hands.

**STABILIZATION IN PROGRESS...**
**PLEASE HOLD DETECTOR STEADY.**

I stepped out of the tunnel, holding the detector in front of me like a shield.

Dr. Finch was standing twenty feet away, his eyes locked on me.

"What are you doing?" he said.

"Closing the wormhole," I said. "Like Marvin wanted."

"No!" Finch started running toward me. "You don't understand! If you close it, we'll lose everything! Decades of research! The chance to explore other dimensions!"

"Or," I said, "we'll save the town from something we can't control."

The detector beeped.

**STABILIZATION COMPLETE.**

**WORMHOLE APERTURE CLOSED.**

There was a sound—a deep, resonant hum that seemed to come from everywhere and nowhere at once.

And then, in the distance, back in the warehouse, there was a flash of light.

The wormhole collapsed.

The shimmer in the air disappeared.

The alien ship—half-visible, caught between worlds—vanished.

Dr. Finch stopped running.

He stood there, staring at the warehouse, his face pale.

"You...you closed it," he said, his voice hollow.

"Yeah," I said. "I did."

He looked at me, and for a moment, I thought he might try to take the detector by force.

But then his shoulders sagged.

"Marvin was right," he said quietly. "He said you'd finish what he started. I didn't believe him."

"What do you mean?" I asked.

Finch sighed. "Before Marvin died, he came to me. He said he'd hidden the wormhole and left clues for someone to find. Someone who would do the right thing. I thought he was delusional. But he wasn't."

He turned and started walking away, his footsteps slow and heavy.

"Where are you going?" Raven called.

"Back to Tanthrop Hammond," he said without looking back. "To tell them the wormhole is gone. That Marvin won."

He disappeared into the darkness.

We stood there for a long moment, the silence pressing down on us.

"Is it over?" Siggy asked.

"I think so," I said.

Raven walked over and put a hand on my shoulder. "You did it, Gavin. You closed the wormhole."

"We did it," I said. "All of us."

I looked down at the detector.

The screen had changed:

**MISSION COMPLETE.**
**THANK YOU.**
**—M.M.**

I smiled.

"Marvin," I said quietly. "We did it."

(scene break)

We biked home as the first light of dawn touched the horizon.

The town was still asleep, unaware that we'd just saved it from a threat it would never know existed.

When I got home, I climbed back through my bedroom window, hid the detector under my bed, and collapsed onto my mattress.

I was asleep in seconds.

(scene break)

The next morning, Mom woke me up for breakfast.

"You look exhausted," she said. "Late night?"

"Yeah," I said. "Couldn't sleep."

She gave me a look, but didn't push.

At the table, Lila was chattering about something, and Paige was texting on her phone.

Dad was reading the news on his tablet.

"Hey, Gavin," he said, looking up. "Did you see this?"

He turned the tablet toward me.

The headline read: **TANTHROP HAMMOND SHUTS DOWN WORMHOLE RESEARCH PROJECT.**

Below it was a photo of Dr. Alan Finch standing outside the Tanthrop Hammond headquarters.

I read the article quickly.

*After decades of classified research, Tanthrop Hammond Corporation announced today that it is permanently closing its experimental wormhole research program. Dr. Alan Finch, lead researcher, stated that the project had reached a dead end and would not be pursued further.*

*"We've learned a great deal," Finch said in a statement, "but some doors are better left closed."*

I looked up at Dad.

"Weird, right?" he said. "I worked on that project for a while. Always seemed like science fiction to me."

"Yeah," I said. "Weird."

But I knew the truth.

The wormhole was closed.

The threat was gone.

And Marvin Marindale's final mission was complete.
