CHAPTER 21 - THE ROOF OF MESA VIEW

Mesa View Elementary looked different in the afternoon light.

I hadn't been back since we moved, and standing in front of it now felt strange—like visiting a place from a dream.

The building was three stories tall, made of tan brick with rows of windows that reflected the sky. The playground was empty, the swings swaying gently in the breeze.

Siggy and Raven were waiting for me at the south entrance, just like we'd planned.

"So," Siggy said as I walked up, "how exactly are we getting on the roof?"

"There's a maintenance ladder," I said. "On the back side of the building. It goes all the way up."

"And you know this how?" Raven asked.

I hesitated. "I went to school here. Before we moved."

"Did you go on the roof?" Siggy asked.

"Once," I said. "Last summer."

I didn't tell them why. I didn't tell them about the paper airplane I'd thrown off the roof, or the bet I'd made with myself to see if I could hit the dumpster three stories below.

I didn't tell them that I'd been trying to prove I was brave, even though I felt like everything in my life was falling apart.

"Come on," I said. "Let's go."

We circled around to the back of the building, staying low and keeping an eye out for teachers or janitors. The school day had ended an hour ago, but there were still a few cars in the parking lot—probably staff staying late to grade papers or prep for tomorrow.

The maintenance ladder was right where I remembered it: a metal ladder bolted to the side of the building, leading up to the roof.

"That's it?" Raven asked, staring up at it. "We just...climb?"

"Yep."

"What if someone sees us?"

"Then we say we're looking for a lost frisbee."

"On the roof?"

"It was a really bad throw."

Siggy laughed.

I went first, gripping the cold metal rungs and pulling myself up. The ladder was solid, but my hands were sweating, and I had to force myself not to look down.

One rung at a time.

Second story.

Third story.

And then I was at the top, pulling myself over the edge onto the flat roof.

The view hit me immediately.

From up here, you could see everything. The playground below. The neighborhood stretching out in every direction. And in the distance, the mountains, hazy and blue against the horizon.

Siggy climbed up next, breathing hard. "Whoa."

Raven came up last, her expression unreadable. She stood at the edge of the roof and looked out over the town.

"This is amazing," she said quietly.

"Yeah," I said.

For a moment, we just stood there, taking it in.

Then Siggy said, "So where's the clue?"

I pulled out my phone and checked the coordinates again.

The pin was right on top of us.

"It's here somewhere," I said. "Marvin's message said 'where knowledge reaches the sky.' The roof has to be it."

We spread out, searching.

The roof was mostly flat, covered in tar and gravel, with a few vents and pipes sticking up. There was an old air conditioning unit in one corner, humming quietly.

"Anything?" Raven called.

"Not yet," I said.

I walked along the edge of the roof, scanning the ground.

And then I saw it.

A scrap of paper, wedged under the base of one of the vents.

It was folded into the shape of a paper airplane.

My heart skipped a beat.

I knelt down and carefully pulled it free.

It was old, the paper yellowed and brittle, but it was definitely a paper airplane. And on one of the wings, written in faint blue ink, was a message:

**LOOK BELOW.**

I stood up and walked to the edge of the roof, looking down.

Three stories below, near the base of the building, there was a narrow gap between the school and the old equipment shed. The kind of space that probably never got cleaned, filled with weeds and forgotten junk.

"Guys," I said. "I think I found it."

Siggy and Raven came over.

"What is it?" Raven asked.

"The clue says to look below." I pointed down at the gap. "I think the next piece is down there."

"How are we supposed to get down there?" Siggy asked.

I thought about it. The gap was too narrow to walk through from ground level—you'd have to squeeze between the buildings. But from up here...

"We throw something," I said.

"Throw what?"

I looked at the paper airplane in my hand.

"This."

Raven frowned. "How is that going to help?"

"It's not," I said. "But maybe it'll show us where to look."

I unfolded the airplane carefully, smoothing out the creases.

On the inside of the paper, hidden beneath the folds, was another message:

**THE THIRD PIECE LIES IN THE SHADOW. COORDINATES: 35.0844° N, 106.6505° W.**

**RETRIEVE IT CAREFULLY. THEY ARE WATCHING.**

I showed the message to Siggy and Raven.

"Coordinates again," Raven said. "Let me check."

She typed them into her phone.

"It's...right below us," she said. "In that gap."

"So we need to get down there," I said.

We climbed back down the ladder, my legs shaking slightly from the adrenaline.

At ground level, we circled around to the gap between the school and the shed.

It was narrow—maybe two feet wide—and filled with overgrown weeds, broken bricks, and garbage that had blown in over the years.

"I'll go," I said.

"Are you sure?" Raven asked.

"Yeah. I'm the smallest."

That wasn't true—Raven was definitely smaller than me—but I didn't want to admit that I felt responsible. This was my old school. My roof. My mess to clean up.

I squeezed into the gap, turning sideways to fit between the walls.

The ground was uneven, and I had to step carefully to avoid tripping over bricks and chunks of concrete. The air smelled like dirt and mildew.

I pulled out my phone and turned on the flashlight.

The narrow beam of light cut through the shadows, illuminating the debris.

And there, tucked into a crevice in the foundation of the school, was another metal box.

I reached for it, my fingers brushing against the cold metal.

As I pulled it free, I heard a sound.

Footsteps.

I froze.

The footsteps were coming from the parking lot, just around the corner of the building.

"Gavin!" Siggy hissed. "Someone's coming!"

I shoved the box into my backpack and squeezed back out of the gap as fast as I could.

Raven grabbed my arm and pulled me toward the edge of the playground.

We ran.

Behind us, I heard a man's voice. "Hey! You kids aren't supposed to be here!"

We didn't stop.

We ran across the playground, past the swings and the monkey bars, and didn't slow down until we were two blocks away, hidden behind a row of parked cars.

I bent over, gasping for breath.

"Did he follow us?" I panted.

Siggy peered around the car. "I don't think so."

Raven was breathing hard, her face flushed. "Was that a teacher?"

"I don't know," I said. "Maybe. Or maybe..."

I didn't finish the sentence.

But we were all thinking it.

*They are watching.*

We regrouped at a park a few blocks away, sitting on a bench under a tree.

I pulled the metal box out of my backpack and set it on the bench between us.

It was the same as the others. Rusted. Latched. Heavy.

I opened it.

Inside was the third device—this one more complex than the first two, with a small screen and a series of buttons on one side. And beneath it, another letter.

I unfolded it and read aloud:

---

*You are doing well.*

*Three pieces found. Only one remains.*

*The fourth and final piece—the core—is the most important. Without it, the machine will not function. But it is also the most dangerous to retrieve.*

*The core is hidden in Hammerstrike, my former home. To reach it, you must enter the estate and find the Orrery Room.*

*But you cannot simply walk in. The estate is locked, and the locks are designed to keep out those who do not belong.*

*To unlock the estate, you must complete all remaining challenges in the game. When you do, you will receive the final code.*

*And when the solar event arrives—the autumn equinox at midnight—the Orrery will activate. Use it wisely.*

*But be warned: you are not the only ones seeking the core. There are those who will do anything to stop you.*

*Stay together. Trust each other. And remember: the truth is closer than you think.*

*—M.M.*

---

I finished reading and looked up.

"Hammerstrike," Siggy said. "That's Marvin Marindale's mansion, right?"

"Yeah," I said. "Grandpa told me about it. It's the old Brimley estate. Marvin lived there until he died."

"And we're supposed to break into it," Raven said.

"Not break in," I said. "Unlock it. With the code from the game."

"How many challenges are left?" Siggy asked.

I thought about it. "We've done seven. I don't know how many there are total."

"Then we need to keep playing," Raven said.

I packed the third device carefully into my backpack, along with the letter.

"Autumn equinox," I said. "When is that?"

Raven pulled out her phone and typed. "September 22nd. At midnight."

"That's less than two weeks away," Siggy said.

"Then we don't have much time," I said.

We sat there for a moment, the weight of it all sinking in.

Three pieces of a machine.

One more to find.

And less than two weeks to do it.

That night, I set up the Raspberry Pi and loaded the game.

**CHALLENGE 8: LOCKED.**

I clicked on it.

The screen changed, showing a new puzzle.

This time, it wasn't a maze or a triangle or a sequence.

It was a grid.

A 5x5 grid filled with numbers, some of them highlighted.

At the bottom, a message appeared:

**CHALLENGE 8: FIND THE MAGIC SQUARE.**

**ARRANGE THE NUMBERS SO THAT EVERY ROW, COLUMN, AND DIAGONAL ADDS UP TO THE SAME SUM.**

I stared at the screen.

A magic square.

I'd heard of them before—ancient puzzles where you had to arrange numbers in a grid so that all the rows and columns added up to the same total.

This was going to take a while.

I grabbed my notebook and got to work.
