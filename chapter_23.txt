CHAPTER 23 - THE FINAL CHALLENGES

Saturday morning arrived with gray skies and a cold wind that rattled the windows.

I was up before sunrise, too anxious to sleep.

Today was the STEM Fair.

Today, we'd find the fourth piece.

I got dressed, grabbed my backpack (with all three device pieces carefully wrapped inside), and headed downstairs.

Mom was already in the kitchen, making coffee.

"Big day," she said, smiling. "You nervous?"

"A little."

"You'll do great. Grandpa helped you build that robot, right?"

"Yeah."

"Then it'll be amazing." She poured me a glass of orange juice. "What time do you need to be there?"

"Nine. Setup starts at nine."

"I'll drive you."

"Thanks, Mom."

She looked at me for a long moment, and I could see something in her expression—pride, maybe, or relief. Like she was glad to see me doing something positive after all the anger and frustration of the past year.

"I'm proud of you, Gavin," she said quietly.

My throat tightened.

"Thanks," I said.

(scene break)

Hammerstrike Estate sat on a hill overlooking the town, surrounded by tall iron gates and old trees.

The mansion itself was massive—three stories of stone and wood, with a round turret on one side and wide windows that gleamed in the morning light.

Marvin Marindale's home.

Mom dropped me off at the front gate, where a security guard checked my name off a list and handed me a badge.

"Setup is in the Grand Hall," he said. "Follow the signs."

I walked up the long driveway, my heart pounding.

The grounds were immaculate. There were gardens, fountains, and a huge lawn that stretched out behind the mansion. A banner hung over the front entrance: **WELCOME TO THE MARINDALE STEM FAIR.**

Inside, the Grand Hall was buzzing with activity.

Kids were setting up tables, arranging display boards, and testing their projects. Teachers and parents milled around, offering help and encouragement.

I found Siggy and Raven near the back of the hall, at a table labeled **PROJECT 27: BLEEPBLORP.**

"Gavin!" Siggy said. "Dude, this place is insane."

"I know," I said, looking around.

The hall was enormous, with high ceilings, chandeliers, and wood-paneled walls. At the far end, a grand staircase led up to the second floor.

That's where we needed to go.

"Have you seen anyone...suspicious?" I asked quietly.

Raven shook her head. "Not yet. But there are a lot of people here."

"Let's set up fast," I said. "Then we find the Orrery Room."

We unpacked the robot, the display board, and the poster board with the black line. I set the robot on the line and turned it on.

It wobbled forward, following the path perfectly.

A few kids nearby stopped to watch.

"Whoa, that's cool," one of them said.

"Thanks," I said, trying to act casual.

But inside, I was buzzing with nerves.

(scene break)

By 10 AM, the fair was in full swing.

Judges walked around with clipboards, stopping at each project to ask questions. Parents and younger siblings wandered through, pointing and taking photos.

Our robot performed flawlessly. It followed the line, avoided obstacles, and even completed a simple maze we'd built out of cardboard.

Ms. Ehlo stopped by and grinned. "This is fantastic, Gavin. You three did a great job."

"Thanks," I said.

She moved on to the next table.

As soon as she was out of earshot, I turned to Siggy and Raven.

"Now," I said.

We slipped away from the table, weaving through the crowd toward the grand staircase.

No one stopped us.

We climbed to the second floor, where the hallway was quieter. There were fewer people up here—mostly just staff setting up refreshments for later.

"Where's the Orrery Room?" Raven whispered.

"I don't know," I said. "But Marvin's letter said it's in the turret."

We found a narrow hallway that led toward the round turret we'd seen from outside.

At the end of the hallway was a door.

It was locked.

There was a keypad beside it, with a small screen that read: **ENTER CODE.**

"This is it," I said.

I pulled out my phone and opened the game.

**CHALLENGE 10: LOCKED.**

"Wait," Siggy said. "We haven't solved the last two challenges yet."

"Then we solve them now."

I clicked on Challenge 10.

The screen changed, showing a word problem:

**CHALLENGE 10: A TRAIN LEAVES STATION A AT 60 MPH. ANOTHER TRAIN LEAVES STATION B, 180 MILES AWAY, AT 40 MPH. WHEN WILL THEY MEET?**

I grabbed my notebook and started scribbling.

The trains are moving toward each other, so their speeds add up: 60 + 40 = 100 mph.

The distance between them is 180 miles.

Time = Distance ÷ Speed.

Time = 180 ÷ 100 = 1.8 hours.

I entered **1.8** into the game.

**CHALLENGE 10: COMPLETE.**

The screen flashed, and the next challenge appeared.

**CHALLENGE 11: THE FINAL CHALLENGE.**

**SOLVE FOR X:**

**3X + 7 = 22.**

This one was easy.

3X + 7 = 22
3X = 15
X = 5

I entered **5** into the game.

The screen flashed.

**CHALLENGE 11: COMPLETE.**

**ALL CHALLENGES SOLVED.**

**THE CODE IS: 1-7-8-9.**

I stared at the screen.

1-7-8-9.

"Got it," I said.

I walked over to the keypad and entered the code.

The screen flashed green.

The door clicked open.

(scene break)

The Orrery Room was breathtaking.

The round chamber was filled with light from tall windows that overlooked the town. In the center of the room was the orrery—a mechanical model of the solar system, with brass arms and spheres representing the planets, all rotating slowly around a central sun.

And in the floor, directly beneath the orrery, was a spiral-shaped indentation.

The place where the scepter goes, I thought.

"Whoa," Siggy whispered.

Raven walked over to the orrery, examining it closely. "This is incredible."

"Look for the fourth piece," I said.

We spread out, searching the room.

There were shelves lined with books, a desk covered in papers, and a telescope pointed out one of the windows.

And in the corner, behind a stack of old journals, was a small wooden box.

I opened it.

Inside was the fourth device—the core.

It was small, no bigger than a walnut, but it glowed faintly with a soft blue light.

Next to it was a note.

---

*You have done well.*

*The four pieces are complete. Assemble them, and the detector will be ready.*

*But the detector alone is not enough. You must use it in conjunction with the Orrery and the scepter.*

*The scepter is hidden beneath the foundation of this estate, in the place where fire once forged steel.*

*Find it. And on the night of the autumn equinox, when the stars align, return to this room.*

*Place the scepter in the spiral. Activate the detector. And the truth will be revealed.*

*Good luck.*

*—M.M.*

---

I read the note aloud.

"The place where fire once forged steel," Raven said. "What does that mean?"

"Brimley's Forge," I said. "Hammerstrike used to be part of the forge complex. There must be an old foundry somewhere on the grounds."

"We need to find it," Siggy said.

"But not now," I said. "We need to get back to the fair before anyone notices we're gone."

I packed the fourth piece carefully into my backpack.

And then I heard footsteps in the hallway.

We froze.

The footsteps stopped outside the door.

A voice—low and cold—said, "I know you're in there."

It was him.

The man in the dark jacket.

(scene break)

My heart was pounding so hard I thought it might burst out of my chest.

Raven looked at me, her eyes wide with fear.

Siggy mouthed, *What do we do?*

I looked around the room.

There was only one door—the one the man was standing outside of.

But there were windows.

Tall windows that opened onto a narrow ledge.

"The window," I whispered.

We ran to the nearest window and pushed it open.

The ledge outside was maybe a foot wide, running along the outside of the turret.

"You've got to be kidding," Siggy said.

"Go!" I hissed.

Raven climbed out first, pressing herself against the wall. I went next, my legs shaking.

Siggy came last, just as the door to the Orrery Room burst open.

The man stepped inside, his eyes scanning the room.

We edged along the ledge, moving as quietly as we could.

Below us, the ground was three stories down.

Don't look down, I told myself.

We reached another window—this one leading into a different room—and I pushed it open.

We climbed inside, landing in a dusty storage room filled with old furniture and boxes.

"Come on," I said.

We ran.

Down a hallway. Down the stairs. Through the Grand Hall, weaving between tables and projects.

We reached our table, breathing hard, just as Ms. Ehlo walked by.

"Everything okay?" she asked.

"Yeah," I said, forcing a smile. "Just...needed some fresh air."

She nodded and moved on.

I looked at Siggy and Raven.

We'd done it.

We had all four pieces.

Now we just had to survive long enough to use them.

(end of chapter 23)
